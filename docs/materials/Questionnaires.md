# Questionnaires (as used in the app)

Generated from `study/content.py` and `study/views.py`, so it always matches what participants see. This replaces the earlier draft `self-efficacy, difficulty, and realism questionnaire.md`. Use this file as the paper's appendix and as a printed backup if the laptop fails.

Why these items: self-efficacy is measured for the task (Bandura's guide), because general self-efficacy items describe a trait and barely change between blocks. Difficulty is the manipulation check (expected C < A <= F). One realism item per block keeps three blocks short; the turn tags give the detail.

## 1. Pre-session (in the app)

### Background

- Age
- Gender: Woman / Man / Non-binary / Prefer not to say
- Program: Master's / PhD
- Year in program
- First language
- How comfortable are you speaking English with professors? (1 = Not at all comfortable, 7 = Very comfortable)
- How often have you had to ask an advisor or professor for something difficult? (1 = Never, 7 = Very often)
- How often do you use AI chatbots (e.g., ChatGPT, Gemini)?: Never / A few times a month / Weekly / Daily

### Baseline: STAI-6 (Marteau and Bekker, 1992)

Instruction: choose how you feel **right now**. Scale: 1 = Not at all, 2 = Somewhat, 3 = Moderately, 4 = Very much.

1. I feel calm (reverse scored)
2. I am tense
3. I feel upset
4. I am relaxed (reverse scored)
5. I feel content (reverse scored)
6. I am worried

Score: sum after reversing items 1, 4 and 5, then multiply by 20/6 (range 20 to 80).

### Baseline: Self-efficacy (1 = Not at all confident, 7 = Completely confident)

1. I am confident I could make a difficult request to my real advisor.
2. I am confident I could stay calm if my advisor pushed back.
3. I am confident I could keep asking for what I need, even if my advisor disagreed at first.
4. I am confident I could propose a clear plan when asked.

Score: mean of the four items. The same wording is used at baseline and after every block, so scores are comparable.

## 2. After each block (three times)

Header: think about the conversations you just had with the [colour] Dr. Lee.

### Block: STAI-6 (Marteau and Bekker, 1992)

Instruction: choose how you feel **right now**. Scale: 1 = Not at all, 2 = Somewhat, 3 = Moderately, 4 = Very much.

2. I am tense
3. I feel upset
4. I am relaxed (reverse scored)
5. I feel content (reverse scored)
6. I am worried

Score: sum after reversing items 1, 4 and 5, then multiply by 20/6 (range 20 to 80).

### Block: Self-efficacy (1 = Not at all confident, 7 = Completely confident)

1. I am confident I could make a difficult request to my real advisor.
2. I am confident I could stay calm if my advisor pushed back.
3. I am confident I could keep asking for what I need, even if my advisor disagreed at first.
4. I am confident I could propose a clear plan when asked.

Score: mean of the four items. The same wording is used at baseline and after every block, so scores are comparable.

### Block ratings (1 to 7)

- **difficulty**: In the practice rounds, how difficult was Dr. Lee to talk to? (1 = Very easy, 7 = Very difficult)
- **realism**: In the practice rounds, how realistic was Dr. Lee as an advisor? (1 = Not at all realistic, 7 = Very realistic)

### Turn tags (on each of Dr. Lee's practice turns, optional)

Tags: unrealistic, too harsh, too easy, helpful, plus an optional one-line reason.

### Between practice rounds

- How stressed do you feel right now? (0 = Not at all stressed, 10 = Extremely stressed)
- Optional: Next round starts fresh. Is there one thing you want to try differently?

## 3. After the human role-play

### Role-play: STAI-6 (Marteau and Bekker, 1992)

Instruction: choose how you feel **right now**. Scale: 1 = Not at all, 2 = Somewhat, 3 = Moderately, 4 = Very much.

1. I feel calm (reverse scored)
2. I am tense
3. I feel upset
4. I am relaxed (reverse scored)
5. I feel content (reverse scored)
6. I am worried

Score: sum after reversing items 1, 4 and 5, then multiply by 20/6 (range 20 to 80).

- Compared with the AI, how stressful was talking to the real person? (1 = Much less stressful, 7 = Much more stressful)

## 4. End of session

- Rank the three colour-labelled Dr. Lees from most helpful (1) to least helpful (3).
- Interview (researcher asks, notes typed on the monitor page):
  - Which Dr. Lee helped you most, and why?
  - When did Dr. Lee stop feeling real?
  - Did practicing with the AI help with the real person?
